# THESIS CHAPTER MAP

## Part I — Current approaches for antigen discovery in cancer

### Purpose
Provide the conceptual and technical framework required to understand why comprehensive cancer antigen discovery remains challenging.

### Draft structure
1. Cancer antigens: canonical, non-canonical, tumor-specific and shared antigenic spaces
2. Transcriptomic approaches to antigen discovery
3. Genomic and variant-derived antigen discovery
4. Immunopeptidomics and direct evidence of HLA presentation
5. Long-read sequencing and expanded transcript/ORF space
6. Computational prediction and antigen prioritization
7. Validation strategies and major limitations
8. Emerging integrated multi-omics strategies
9. Unresolved gaps motivating the pan-cancer application

## Part II — Application of a cutting-edge antigen discovery platform in a large pan-cancer cohort

### Purpose
Demonstrate how the platform operates at scale and what the integrated dataset reveals about cancer antigen space.

### Draft structure
1. Rationale and study design
2. Cohort and modality landscape
3. Platform architecture and analytical workflow
4. Candidate antigen discovery across tumor types
5. Filtering/prioritization/validation strategy
6. Pan-cancer distribution and biological patterns
7. Key examples / representative antigen classes
8. Limitations and implications
9. Transition: discovery identifies candidates, but candidate quality can still limit downstream utility

The exact analytical content must be determined from the completed sample × analysis inventory and validated outputs.

## Part III — Optimization of antigen candidates with mimotopes

### Purpose
Develop and evaluate a computational strategy to optimize selected antigen candidates, connecting candidate discovery to experimentally testable engineered sequences.

### Draft structure
1. Rationale for antigen optimization
2. Mimotope design space and candidate generation
3. Computational scoring / binding / structural criteria
4. Candidate prioritization and selection
5. Structural analysis and mechanistic interpretation
6. Experimental validation framework
7. Pending immunogenicity experiments and what cannot yet be concluded
8. Integrated interpretation and limitations

## Cross-part synthesis

The overarching logic should be:

**Understand the antigen-discovery landscape → expand and systematically interrogate antigen space at pan-cancer scale → optimize selected candidates for downstream use.**
